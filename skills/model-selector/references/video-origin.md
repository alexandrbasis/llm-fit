## Message and questions

At [00:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9weiIHy9T_0&t=45), the author asks: “Do you know what benchmarks matter most for your work and why?” At [16:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9weiIHy9T_0&t=973): “What am I optimizing for?”

He asks viewers to prioritize a small benchmark set, examine quality/cost/speed, and consider honesty, constraint following, long work, coordination and recovery. His five choices reflect his ambition for autonomous engineering systems; they are not a universal task taxonomy. He promotes a model stack and his education business. The durable lesson is to define useful work before choosing its proxies; specific winners are a dated observation. Our interpretation is that the video teaches constructing a personal evaluation policy, rather than merely reading a leaderboard.

## Boundaries of the adaptation

The skill adds exact platform/configuration verification, an adaptive user interview, noncoding routes, cost per accepted task, and a small local validation recipe. At the user's request it separates quality leaders, value leaders and the cheapest adequate option, and discovers model and benchmark knowledge anew each run. These are our operational extensions, not claims that the author supplied a complete selection procedure.

Do not turn the author's preferences into universal facts: saturated tests can still establish requirements; proxy transfer, improvements from longer prompts, and multi-agent gains need testing. Missing scores do not establish provider bias. Factual error metrics do not establish intentional deception. The [source map](source-map.md) points to live services; the [benchmark-selection method](benchmark-selection.md) explains how to read their current evaluations. No historical ranking or benchmark definition is a permanent selection rule.
