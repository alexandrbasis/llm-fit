# Discover the candidate pool before selecting favorites

Provider-neutral eligibility is not enough: actively check for relevant alternatives before narrowing the pool. Model families, market leaders and benchmark coverage are live facts, never a permanent list in this skill.

## Establish the allowed scope

The user's current app, subscription or model is a baseline, not an exclusive constraint unless the request makes it one. Consider alternative applications, direct APIs, hosted routes and local deployment when compatible with the requested use. A different interface may require a verified integration; mark that condition instead of assuming it works or silently excluding the model.

For an explicit restriction to one application, provider or deployment, discover within that boundary and name it. Otherwise consider task-relevant closed and open-weight models from the broader market, including specialized and economical candidates. Reputation, country of origin and familiarity do not establish task quality or suitability; apply the user's actual availability, privacy, language, licensing and deployment constraints using evidence.

## Run a broad discovery pass

1. Cross-check at least two independently maintained discovery sources suitable for the permitted scope, including a broad catalog rather than only a familiar provider's pages. Use [source-map.md](source-map.md); an evaluation catalog, a multi-provider hosting catalog and publisher model cards reveal different parts of the market. Mirrors of the same list do not add coverage. For a tightly restricted scope, the current authoritative catalog plus relevant task evidence may suffice. If suitable sources are inaccessible, identify the resulting partial coverage.
2. Inspect relevant quality leaders, lower-cost alternatives and specialists found there before selecting the detailed comparison pool. Follow plausible challengers to their current model-owner documentation and task evidence, including models absent from a preferred leaderboard. Listings and popularity establish discovery, not performance.
3. Resolve exact identity, version, owner and access route. The same underlying model hosted by several providers is one model with potentially different routes, prices and settings. An alias, application, subscription or orchestration service is not automatically a distinct standalone model. Verify what a name denotes before assigning it scores.
4. Check candidates against the task and hard constraints. Include economical candidates in the quality comparison when their evidence warrants it; apply the same evidence standard to every origin and license type. Missing benchmark coverage means unknown performance, not demonstrated weakness. Preserve unresolved contenders that could change a winner.

Do not force a certain number of vendors, a regional quota or an open-weight finalist. The purpose is to discover credible competition; the final three may come from one provider when the evidence supports that result.

## Account for names supplied by the user

Investigate every explicitly requested model, family or service. For a family, find its currently relevant variants rather than silently selecting a remembered release. Give each named item one disposition in the working ledger:

`resolved identity / route | considered and supporting evidence | excluded with concrete constraint or comparative reason | unresolved and missing check`

An ambiguous or unverified name stays unresolved. Search official sources first; ask a short identification question only if it materially blocks the comparison, while continuing work on the other candidates. Do not guess an underlying model or forget the item. Being named guarantees consideration, not a place in the top three.

## Check coverage before stopping

The working ledger records the sources searched, candidate identities, plausible challengers, consequential exclusions and unresolved gaps. Finish when the relevant candidates from the broad pass and every user-named item are accounted for, meaningful cheaper alternatives have been investigated, and no discovered challenger that could change a winner has been silently skipped.

This is bounded research, not proof of exhaustive market coverage. Restrict “best” and “cheapest” to the checked eligible pool. If the available evidence covers only a narrow ecosystem, state that scope and keep broader claims unresolved; a disclaimer is not a substitute for a feasible discovery pass.

Keep the four-block answer. Use its shared status line for a material coverage limit; give a short disposition for each user-named candidate missing from the tables within the relevant benchmark or cost block. Keep the full discovery log internal unless requested. Never turn this log into a permanent model list in the skill.
